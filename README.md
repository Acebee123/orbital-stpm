There are no orbital surfaces, outlines, nuclei or nuclear zoom stages in this module. Blue and magenta encode opposite wavefunction phases, not charge. Smooth internal drift is the default visual mode. Particle brightness stays steady. Symmetry rotations preserve radial and angular probability density for s, p and dz2 orbitals; the other d orbitals use a small tangent drift constrained by node distance. Radial shells and angular nodal sectors remain intact. This motion is a teaching illustration, not an electron trajectory. A still-cloud mode is also available. The 1s, 2s and 3s clouds use increasing illustrative radii (1.35, 2.15 and 3.05 display units) on the same viewing scale, with corresponding radial nodes and phases scaled consistently. These are qualitative sizes, not hard physical boundaries.

One scientific coordinate frame puts z upright. dz² has two vertical z lobes and an equatorial ring in xy; both the cloud and labelled axes share this frame. All angular and radial nodes are preserved by the hydrogen-like probability kernel. A qualitative envelope weights the distribution toward the familiar orbital shapes, with a faint short tail. Samples are probability-density samples, not individual electrons or trajectories. This is an educational representation, not a numerical many-electron quantum calculation. Display dimensions are adjusted for clarity; the sample renewal does not imply a time-varying stationary-state probability density.

Drag, scroll or pinch to inspect. The settings zoom slider smoothly dollies while preserving the viewing direction. Reset camera restores the recommended view. Controls use icons and orbital shape glyphs with accessible names, plus scientific notation for every orbital option. A prominent upper-left readout identifies the selected orbital. Colored x, y and z labels appear with the axes. The top switch selects Atomic Orbitals or Hybridization; the floating bottom panel holds selections, playback and settings. ## Hybridisation

Only one s and one, two or three p orbitals enter sp, sp² and sp³. There are no molecular examples, hydrogen partners, nuclei or sigma bonds in this section. Source orbitals reuse the Atomic Orbitals probability sampler. Starting, intermediate and final hybrid shapes are rendered only as 117,040 probability points per orbital. No continuous volume, filled surface or outer shell is rendered. Small individually visible Gaussian points have density-weighted opacity, gently varied sizes and restrained bright centres, with smooth symmetry flow around each local lobe axis and stable opacity. Blue and magenta encode wavefunction phases, not charge. Fuller main lobes and smaller reverse lobes converge at one shared origin, with no atom or nucleus.

The independent 16-second teaching timeline holds the source orbitals in a clean, centred row (or a compact phone arrangement) for three seconds, moves them inward, pauses before mixing, then continuously redistributes and reorients the same probability samples over six seconds using a GPU morph. Final samples follow squared hydrogen-like 2s and direction-aligned 2p combinations, with exact 1/2, 1/3 or 1/4 s character and a qualitative display envelope. Density is concentrated in the body and fades to zero at the edge, with no far-field dust. Display dimensions and reverse-lobe visibility are adjusted for teaching; the points are samples of electron probability density, not separate electrons or measured populations. Four seconds remain to inspect the final equivalent orbitals: linear 180°, trigonal planar 120°, tetrahedral 109.5°. The morph represents linear combinations of wavefunctions; it is not a literal reaction or excitation. Replay, next steps, seeking and speed operate on this hybrid-only timeline.

Camera orbit and scroll/pinch zoom stay enabled during the hybridisation animation. Input immediately yields cinematic guidance without pausing the chemistry. The user's angle survives release, pause/resume, next steps, timeline navigation, replay and hybrid selection. Only Reset View explicitly restores the teaching angle through damping. Playback pauses when the browser tab is hidden.

Sound uses the native Web Audio API. The original noise and oscillator sources, filters and pitch sweeps remain. Separate whoosh, impact and energy GainNodes feed a master GainNode and DynamicsCompressorNode. Sound Volume ramps master gain without clicks. Sound On/Off is separate and gesture-activated. Hybridisation uses movement and settling cues without bond-contact effects.

## Electron Configuration Builder (H–Ca)

Click any of the first 20 elements. The full table fades to 10% behind a selected-element card and editable builder. Each element starts with one empty row and retains its answer while switching. Add s/p/d entries, choose n and occupation, drag options onto rows, reorder using handles or arrow buttons, and remove or clear entries. The electron selector restricts new choices to the selected subshell's capacity; an existing over-capacity value remains marked incorrect until edited. Partial entries stay neutral; completed entries persistently show green ✓ or red ✗ until corrected. Exact ground-state order, electron totals, capacities, Pauli exclusion and Hund's rule are checked. Spin boxes are editable; equivalent p-axis assignments and global spin reversal are accepted. Completion requires the entire configuration, including the correct number of rows. Only then does a separate compact row below the builder show the full configuration and ✓ Complete. It disappears immediately after an error, extra row or missing row. Missing answers are never supplied automatically.

The dismissible Aufbau guide shows diagonal order and capacities. For neutral K/Ca, 4s precedes 3d; the guide distinguishes this from transition-metal ordering. Only neutral atoms 1–20 are supported, using full configurations. d options allow diagnosing mistakes; no d subshell is occupied in these ground states.

View 3D plays reference atomic orbital filling independently of the student's answer; it does not form chemical bonds. Each moving **gold marker represents exactly one electron**, using exactly one rendered point per roster entry with no trails or clones. The separate stationary blue/magenta probability cloud is a smooth volume, not a collection of electron-like dots. A ray-marched analytical field uses the hydrogen-like 1s–4s and 2p/3p radial functions, angular factors and qualitative display envelopes; radial nodes and p nodal planes are preserved. Occupied orbital densities are summed without interference. The field has no time uniform, texture noise or particle motion. Optical intensity and display sizes are illustrative rather than quantitative measurements.

Hydrogen explicitly reads **1s¹**, **1 electron marker**, and “The entire blue cloud is the probability density of ONE electron.” Its distinct coral centre represents the ¹H nucleus (one proton); other elements show a nucleus with charge +Ze without inventing an isotope or neutron count. All nuclear graphics are enlarged for visibility. Gold markers remain visible even when their projections pass the enlarged nucleus. The cloud, electron markers and nucleus have a compact separate-colour legend.

Follow filling, click a subshell, inspect an individual p axis, or select Complete atom. At completion, the whole-atom view has exactly Z moving markers, while a subshell view contains exactly its occupation (and a p-axis view at most two). Markers appear individually in Aufbau order, with Pauli/Hund occupations shown in spin boxes. Their positions use smooth independent interpolation between irregular sampled positions within a nodal cell, preserving radial nodes and p nodal planes. This is an educational counting and probability illustration, not a simulation of identifiable electron trajectories.

Play/Pause controls both filling and marker motion. Motion can continue after filling is complete; Restart explicitly resets the timeline. Seeking, element switching, camera reset, drag rotation and zoom remain available. Manual camera ownership survives scene transitions; Reset View smoothly returns to the teaching angle. Opening the guide pauses playback and closing restores its previous state. The original Atomic Orbitals and Hybridisation renderers are unchanged.

The self-contained `orbital.html` works offline in a WebGL-capable browser. The export script also writes `dist/orbital.html` for download from a deployment. No APIs, remote fonts or paid services are used at runtime.
