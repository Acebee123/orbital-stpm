# Orbital — STPM Chemistry Topic 3

Premium React Three Fiber / Three.js learning flow: Atomic Orbitals → Hybridisation → Orbital Overlap → Bond Formation → Molecular Geometry.

Run `pnpm install`, then `pnpm dev`. Build with `pnpm build`; verify scientific model invariants with `pnpm test`.

For the single-file browser version, open `orbital.html`. It contains the same production JavaScript, CSS, 3D scenes and generated sounds, with no runtime installation needed. Google Fonts is optional; system fonts are used when offline. WebGL must be available in the browser. Recreate the HTML after source changes with `pnpm export:html`, or `node scripts/export-html.mjs` after installing dependencies. A custom output path may be passed as the first argument. The React source and normal build remain available for continued development.

Atomic Orbitals provides 1s / 2s / 3s, 2px / 2py / 2pz and the five 3d orbitals. Rotate, zoom, reset, toggle axes and labels, open s-orbital radial cutaways, and click a surface to highlight it. Geometry vertices and phase colours interpolate smoothly between selections. The s radial nodes use hydrogenic roots. Angular orientations, nodal sectors and phases are checked by the tests.

Scroll, pinch or use the Orbital / Density / Nucleus / Structure controls to explore progressively. Four stages follow deliberate zoom input: clean orbital (0–35%) → probability density (35–70%) → nucleus approach (70–85%) → magnified nuclear structure (85–100%). The camera slows on entry to the broad density range. One uninterrupted scroll, pinch or slider drag cannot carry past 69%; it stops there until the distribution is visible and another deliberate gesture arrives. Nucleus/Structure pressed from the normal view also stops at density first; press again after observing to move deeper. Density is a stable viewing stop, with no timed continuation. Only amplitude shimmer changes; sample positions and nodes stay fixed. The default view shows the probability boundary and central nucleus with no inner density particles. Protons and neutrons remain hidden in the close nucleus view. Deeper zoom smoothly changes to an explicitly labelled educational magnification and reveals six protons and six neutrons. Zooming out reverses the same stages. Click a nucleon to inspect its charge; Motion freezes the gentle sample intensity waves and nuclear vibration. Sample positions stay fixed, so no trajectories or nodal-plane crossings appear. Dragging interrupts guided zoom, and Reset restores the full orbital view.

Choose Explore hybridisation to continue using the same s/p source geometry. Only 2s and 2p enter the sp / sp² / sp³ combinations. The existing morph, cinematic camera, sequential H approach, whoosh/contact/energy sounds, orbital overlap and geometry reveal are preserved. Bond Formation navigation starts at the approach stage. Orbit and scroll/pinch zoom remain enabled throughout playback. Any camera gesture immediately yields cinematic guidance without pausing formation. The chosen angle survives release, pause/resume, next steps, timeline navigation, replay and hybridisation selection. Only Reset View returns to the recommended teaching angle, through damping. When necessary, the final geometry reveal can gently pull back along the existing viewing direction after a quiet interval; it never changes that angle, zooms closer, or competes with input during the reveal. Contact impulses remain subtle, including in a manually inspected view. Sound is gesture-activated; the animation pauses when the browser tab is hidden.

These are qualitative probability boundary diagrams, not a numerical quantum simulation. s boundaries illustrate radial phase regions; displayed dimensions are adjusted for clarity. Transitions are visual interpolations, not literal excitation or time-resolved hybridisation. Phase colours represent wavefunction sign, not charge. The overall phase sign is arbitrary. Bond directions and mutual angles are exact; the final tetrahedral angle is rounded to 109.5°.

Density samples are not individual electrons or trajectories. The hydrogen-like |ψ|² kernel has a smooth display-envelope weight fitted to the retained spherical, ellipsoidal, pinched-lobe and toroidal surfaces. Most samples lie inside, with a small fading tail; radial and angular nodes remain zeros of the kernel. This illustrates orbital shape, including unoccupied orbitals; it is not a quantitative many-electron carbon calculation. Stationary-state probability density is time-independent, so coherent intensity waves are an illustrative effect. Nuclear and orbital sizes use independent display scales. Nucleon positions are a teaching schematic, not a fixed nuclear lattice.

Motion clarity uses a shared teaching timeline: three seconds to inspect the source orbitals, inward gathering, a short still moment, a six-second deformation and rotation into hybrids, and a two-second settled hold. Each hydrogen starts at a display distance of 9.2, accelerates and brakes toward contact, holds for 0.4 seconds, then overlaps. Density visibly builds for more than a second before the sigma bond appears; each bond rests before the next approach. The final rigid molecule rotates slowly as a whole, preserving all mutual angles, then holds still for 2.6 seconds. Camera orbit and zoom remain independent of these events. Atomic s, p and d boundaries retain a stable size and shape. Only a subtle material shimmer and independent fading of fixed probability samples animate the explorer; radial and angular nodes stay in place. Obvious surface deformation, density redistribution and compression are reserved for hybridisation and bond formation. These effects and nuclear vibration are teaching cues; they are not literal time-varying stationary wavefunctions or nuclear trajectories. Normal orbital view still has no inner particles.
