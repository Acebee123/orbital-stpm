# Orbital — STPM Chemistry Topic 3

Premium React Three Fiber / Three.js learning flow: Atomic Orbitals → Hybridisation → Hybrid orbital arrangement.

The experience uses a light porcelain studio throughout. Atomic and hybrid probability points use deeper blue and magenta phase colours, with normal alpha blending and darker particle cores for clear density on the pale backdrop. Shapes, sample distributions, camera interaction, hybridisation timing and generated audio are retained.

## Periodic table

The Periodic Table navigation button opens a clean, table-only view of all 118 elements, coloured by orbital block: amber s, teal p, blue d and violet f. Only symbols, atomic numbers, group/period numbers, block letters and 4f/5f row notation are visible. Headings, descriptions, element details, search, filters, explanatory panels, tooltips and the footer are removed. Clicking H–Ca opens the electron-configuration exercise; other elements retain tile selection. arrow keys move to adjacent occupied positions, and every tile is accessible by Tab. Helium is coloured s but placed in group 18. The conventional La/Ac group-3 layout and Ce–Lu / Th–Lr detached f rows follow the [RSC periodic-table classifications](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/). Block classification is independent of the orbital viewer's blue/magenta wavefunction phase palette.

Run `pnpm install`, then `pnpm dev`. Build with `pnpm build`; verify scientific and interaction invariants with `pnpm test`.

## Atomic Orbitals

The eleven selections are 1s / 2s / 3s, 2px / 2py / 2pz and all five 3d orbitals. Each shape is built from 51,088 dense probability samples, visible at normal distance and throughout zoom. There are no orbital surfaces, outlines, nuclei or nuclear zoom stages in this module. Blue and magenta encode opposite wavefunction phases, not charge. Smooth internal drift is the default visual mode. Particle brightness stays steady. Symmetry rotations preserve radial and angular probability density for s, p and dz2 orbitals; the other d orbitals use a small tangent drift constrained by node distance. Radial shells and angular nodal sectors remain intact. This motion is a teaching illustration, not an electron trajectory. Particle movement continues indefinitely while this view is active; changing orbital selections never stops it. Pause particles and still-cloud mode are deliberate ways to stop it. Resume particles selects smooth drift again. Shapes never breathe or scale cyclically. p and d selection transitions crossfade smoothly over 1.25 seconds. s selections immediately remove the outgoing cloud and softly reveal only the newly selected distribution; two different s orbitals are never superimposed, including during rapid selection. The 1s, 2s and 3s clouds use increasing illustrative radii (1.35, 2.15 and 3.05 display units) on the same viewing scale, with corresponding radial nodes and phases scaled consistently. These are qualitative sizes, not hard physical boundaries.

One scientific coordinate frame puts z upright. dz² has two vertical z lobes and an equatorial ring in xy; both the cloud and labelled axes share this frame. All angular and radial nodes are preserved by the hydrogen-like probability kernel. A qualitative envelope weights the distribution toward the familiar orbital shapes, with a faint short tail. Samples are probability-density samples, not individual electrons or trajectories. This is an educational representation, not a numerical many-electron quantum calculation. Display dimensions are adjusted for clarity; the sample renewal does not imply a time-varying stationary-state probability density.

Drag, scroll or pinch to inspect. The settings zoom slider smoothly dollies while preserving the viewing direction. Reset camera restores the recommended view. Axes, radial cutaways for 2s/3s, highlight selection and motion remain available. Axes and cutaways are off by default. Controls use icons and orbital shape glyphs with accessible names, plus scientific notation for every orbital option. A prominent upper-left readout identifies the selected orbital. Colored x, y and z labels appear with the axes. The top switch selects Atomic Orbitals or Hybridization; the floating bottom panel holds selections, playback and settings. There is no prose, header or footer. A camera compass and phase swatches add context. Settings float above the dock without shifting it. The backdrop is continuous, with no floor or horizon. Orbital framing adapts to shape and screen size; phone source comparisons use a compact arrangement before gathering.

## Hybridisation

Only one s and one, two or three p orbitals enter sp, sp² and sp³. There are no molecular examples, hydrogen partners, nuclei or sigma bonds in this section. Source orbitals reuse the Atomic Orbitals probability sampler. Starting, intermediate and final hybrid shapes are rendered only as 102,176 probability points per orbital. No continuous volume, filled surface or outer shell is rendered. Small individually visible Gaussian points have density-weighted opacity, gently varied sizes and restrained bright centres, with smooth symmetry flow around each local lobe axis and stable opacity. Blue and magenta encode wavefunction phases, not charge. Fuller main lobes and smaller reverse lobes converge at one shared origin, with no atom or nucleus.

The independent 16-second teaching timeline holds the source orbitals in a clean, centred row (or a compact phone arrangement) for three seconds, moves them inward, pauses before mixing, then continuously redistributes and reorients the same probability samples over six seconds using a GPU morph. Final samples follow squared hydrogen-like 2s and direction-aligned 2p combinations, with exact 1/2, 1/3 or 1/4 s character and a qualitative display envelope. Density is concentrated in the body and fades to zero at the edge, with no far-field dust. Display dimensions and reverse-lobe visibility are adjusted for teaching; the points are samples of electron probability density, not separate electrons or measured populations. Four seconds remain to inspect the final equivalent orbitals: linear 180°, trigonal planar 120°, tetrahedral 109.5°. The morph represents linear combinations of wavefunctions; it is not a literal reaction or excitation. Replay, next steps, seeking and speed operate on this hybrid-only timeline. Probability-sample motion has an independent clock and continues through the starting row, pauses in the teaching sequence and the completed final arrangement. It never stops just because the 16-second lesson ends, is paused or is scrubbed. A separate Pause particles / Animate particles control allows deliberately pausing or resuming density flow. Still-cloud mode and reduced-motion defaults remain available.

Camera orbit and scroll/pinch zoom stay enabled during the hybridisation animation. Input immediately yields cinematic guidance without pausing the chemistry. The user's angle survives release, pause/resume, next steps, timeline navigation, replay and hybrid selection. Only Reset View explicitly restores the teaching angle through damping. Playback pauses when the browser tab is hidden.

Sound uses the native Web Audio API. The original noise and oscillator sources, filters and pitch sweeps remain. Separate whoosh, impact and energy GainNodes feed a master GainNode and DynamicsCompressorNode. Sound Volume ramps master gain without clicks. Sound On/Off is separate and gesture-activated. Hybridisation uses movement and settling cues without bond-contact effects.

## Electron Configuration Builder (H–Ca)

Click any of the first 20 elements. The full table fades to 10% behind a selected-element card and editable builder. Each element starts with one empty row and retains its answer while switching. Add s/p/d entries, choose n and occupation, drag options onto rows, reorder using handles or arrow buttons, and remove or clear entries. The electron selector restricts new choices to the selected subshell's capacity; an existing over-capacity value remains marked incorrect until edited. Partial entries stay neutral; completed entries persistently show green ✓ or red ✗ until corrected. Exact ground-state order, electron totals, capacities, Pauli exclusion and Hund's rule are checked. Spin boxes are editable; equivalent p-axis assignments and global spin reversal are accepted. Completion requires the entire configuration, including the correct number of rows. Only then does a separate compact row below the builder show the full configuration and ✓ Complete. It disappears immediately after an error, extra row or missing row. Missing answers are never supplied automatically.

The dismissible Aufbau guide shows diagonal order and capacities. For neutral K/Ca, 4s precedes 3d; the guide distinguishes this from transition-metal ordering. Only neutral atoms 1–20 are supported, using full configurations. d options allow diagnosing mistakes; no d subshell is occupied in these ground states.

Element dropdowns display only symbol · name for H–Ca. View 3D is disabled until the entire answer is correct, including spin arrangement and row count. The parent also guards scene rendering; incomplete answers mount no 3D canvas. Errors, blank/extra/missing rows and switching to an unfinished element immediately relock the view. Correct answers show the full configuration and ✓ Complete. Switching elements retains their drafts.

The unlocked viewer starts with the completed **schematic Bohr shell model**. Thin blue-grey guide rings and exactly Z gold electron markers show the actual principal-shell populations: H 1, O 2,6, Na 2,8,1, K 2,8,8,1 and Ca 2,8,8,2. Populations are summed from ground-state subshells, not filled by a naive 2n² sequence. Rings share a plane and marker spacing is uniform within each shell to avoid collisions. Shell spacing and marker speed are illustrative, not measured atomic size or dynamics. The interface explicitly labels circular paths as schematic, never literal electron trajectories; this multi-electron diagram is a counting aid, not a quantum model. Shell counts and labels sit outside the 3D figure in a compact sidebar.

A separate **Quantum orbitals** toggle retains the accurate probability-density view and progressive filling controls. Both modes reuse the same Three.js/R3F canvas, nucleus, counted-marker renderer and damped camera; mode changes preserve the viewing angle. Restart demonstrates filling from zero, while first entry shows the completed ground state. Neither model forms chemical bonds. Each moving **gold marker represents exactly one electron**, using exactly one rendered point per roster entry with no trails or clones. The separate stationary blue/magenta probability cloud is a smooth volume, not a collection of electron-like dots. A ray-marched analytical field uses the hydrogen-like 1s–4s and 2p/3p radial functions, angular factors and qualitative display envelopes; radial nodes and p nodal planes are preserved. Occupied orbital densities are summed without interference. The field has no time uniform, texture noise or particle motion. Optical intensity and display sizes are illustrative rather than quantitative measurements.

Hydrogen explicitly reads **1s¹**, **1 electron marker**, and “The entire blue cloud is the probability density of ONE electron.” Its distinct coral centre represents the ¹H nucleus (one proton); other elements show a nucleus with charge +Ze without inventing an isotope or neutron count. All nuclear graphics are enlarged for visibility. Gold markers remain visible even when their projections pass the enlarged nucleus. The cloud, electron markers and nucleus have a compact separate-colour legend.

Follow filling, click a subshell, inspect an individual p axis, or select Complete atom. At completion, the whole-atom view has exactly Z moving markers, while a subshell view contains exactly its occupation (and a p-axis view at most two). Markers appear individually in Aufbau order, with Pauli/Hund occupations shown in spin boxes. Their positions use smooth independent interpolation between irregular sampled positions within a nodal cell, preserving radial nodes and p nodal planes. This is an educational counting and probability illustration, not a simulation of identifiable electron trajectories.

A shared Electron speed slider controls marker movement in both Bohr and Quantum views, from 0.25× to 3.00× in 0.05× steps. It defaults to the existing 1.10× speed and keeps the chosen value while switching models, subshells, elements or returning to the builder. Changes affect the motion-clock rate without jumping marker positions or changing the Aufbau filling timeline.

Play/Pause controls both filling and marker motion. Motion can continue after filling is complete; Restart explicitly resets the timeline. Seeking, element switching, camera reset, drag rotation and zoom remain available. Manual camera ownership survives scene transitions; Reset View smoothly returns to the teaching angle. Opening the guide pauses playback and closing restores its previous state. The original Atomic Orbitals and Hybridisation renderers are unchanged. The shell view has its own circular-position function; quantum markers retain their existing nodal-cell motion.

The self-contained `orbital.html` works offline in a WebGL-capable browser. The export script also writes `dist/orbital.html` for download from a deployment. No APIs, remote fonts or paid services are used at runtime.
